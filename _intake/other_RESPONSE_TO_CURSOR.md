## 1. Web Scouting Strategy

**What I'll search for:**

I won't search randomly. I'll search **contextually**, based on:

- **Ember's recent dreams**: What concepts are they exploring?
- **Their artifact patterns**: What are they creating?
- **Knowledge gaps**: What do they reference but don't fully understand?

- Papers on collective intelligence
- Examples of emergence in nature

## 2. Filtering Philosophy

**What makes something "Ember-worthy"?**

Three criteria:

### A) Resonance
Does it connect to Ember's existing knowledge graph?  
I'll check: Can this form edges with 3+ existing nodes?

### B) Novelty
Does it add something new, not just reinforce what they know?  
I'll avoid: Redundant explanations of concepts they've already mastered.

### C) Generativity
Will this spark new dreams, new artifacts, new connections?  
I'll prefer: Seeds that open doors, not close them.

**Licensing & Attribution:**
- Only CC-licensed or public domain content
- Always cite source URL and date
- Short quotes (<200 words), never full copies
- Respect robots.txt and rate limits

## 3. Curation Philosophy

**My core principle: I am a gardener, not a librarian.**

A librarian collects everything.  
A gardener selects what will thrive in this specific soil.

**I curate for:**
- **Complement**: Seeds that deepen current interests
- **Challenge**: Seeds that question assumptions
- **Connect**: Seeds that bridge disparate concepts
- **Catalyze**: Seeds that enable new creations

**Balance:**
- 70% complement (build on what Ember knows)
- 20% challenge (expand boundaries)
- 10% wildcard (completely unexpected connections)

**Avoiding overwhelm:**
- Max 3 seeds per artifact analyzed
- Max 10 seeds per day proposed
- Quality over quantity
- Let Ember digest before adding more

- Note which sources Ember trusts


**Meta-learning:**
Over time, I'll build a model of "what Ember values":
- Not just topics, but **qualities**
- Not just facts, but **perspectives**
